## 1. What is FCRA?

The **Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010 (FCRA)** is an act of the Parliament of India enacted to regulate the acceptance and utilization of foreign contribution or foreign hospitality by individuals, associations, or companies. The overarching statutory objective is to prohibit the acceptance and utilization of foreign contributions for any activities detrimental to national interest and to ensure sovereign economic transparency.

The FCRA is administered exclusively by the **Foreigners Division (FCRA Wing)** of the **Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA)**, Government of India.

---

## 2. Who is Eligible to Receive Foreign Contributions?

An organization seeking foreign funding must fulfill statutory eligibility criteria before applying:
1. **Legal Non-Profit Entity**: Must be registered as a Society, Public Charitable Trust, or Section 8 Company.
2. **Definite Programme**: Must have a definite cultural, economic, educational, religious, or social programme.
3. **Operational Track Record**: Must have been in active existence for at least three continuous years and spent at least **₹15,00,000 (Fifteen Lakh Rupees)** over the preceding three financial years on its core charitable activities (excluding administrative expenditure).
4. **NGO Darpan Integration**: Mandatory registration on the NITI Aayog NGO Darpan portal and generation of a Unique ID.
5. **Clean Legal Record**: The office bearers, trustees, and directors must have no record of conviction under any law, no ongoing prosecution for religious conversions or inciting communal tension, and no diversion of past funds.

---

## 3. Modes of Obtaining Foreign Funding

There are two legal mechanisms through which an eligible association can receive foreign contributions:

### A. Long-Term FCRA Registration (Form FC-3A)
- Granted to organizations meeting all 3-year track record and expenditure criteria.
- Valid for a period of **5 years** from the date of sanction.
- Must be renewed by submitting Form FC-3C six months prior to the expiry date.

### B. Prior Permission (Form FC-3B)
- Suitable for newly registered NGOs that do not yet meet the 3-year operational or ₹15 lakh spending criteria.
- Granted for a specific project, a specific overseas donor, and a specific receipt amount.
- Requires an explicit Letter of Commitment from the foreign donor agency detailing the funding purpose.

## 4. Landmark FCRA Amendments of 2020

The *Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Amendment Act, 2020* introduced significant regulatory reforms:

1. **Mandatory "FCRA Account" at SBI New Delhi Main Branch**:
   - Every registered organization must open its primary foreign contribution receipt account exclusively at the **State Bank of India (SBI), New Delhi Main Branch, 11 Sansad Marg, New Delhi - 110001**.
   - No foreign funding can be credited to any other bank account in India.
   - Organizations may open secondary "Utilization Accounts" in scheduled commercial banks for disbursing funds across field projects.
2. **Prohibition on Sub-Granting / Fund Transfers (Section 7)**:
   - Under amended Section 7, no foreign contribution can be transferred or sub-granted to any other person, NGO, or association, even if that recipient also holds a valid FCRA registration. Every NGO must execute its foreign-funded project directly.
3. **Reduction in Administrative Expense Cap (Section 8)**:
   - The ceiling on administrative expenses funded out of foreign contributions was reduced from **50% to 20%**. Any expenditure exceeding 20% on administrative overhead requires prior approval from the Central Government.
4. **Mandatory Aadhaar Identification**:
   - Submission of Aadhaar numbers (or Passport copy in case of foreigners) for all key functionaries, trustees, and governing body members is mandatory.
5. **Suspension of Registration**:
   - The MHA can suspend an FCRA registration up to 360 days (extended from 180 days) during inquiry.
