## 1. Statutory Forms of NGO Registration in India

Under Indian law, a Non-Governmental Organization (NGO) can be legally incorporated under any one of three primary legal forms. Each form carries distinct structural characteristics, procedural requirements, and regulatory authorities:

| Parameter | 1. Trust (Public Charitable Trust) | 2. Society (Registered Society) | 3. Section 8 Company (Non-Profit Company) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Governing Statute** | *Indian Trusts Act, 1882* (Private) / State Public Trusts Acts (e.g. Bombay Public Trusts Act, 1950) | *Societies Registration Act, 1860* (Act XXI of 1860) or State Societies Acts | *Companies Act, 2013* (Section 8) [formerly Section 25, Companies Act 1956] |
| **Minimum Members Required** | Minimum 2 Trustees (Settlor + Trustee) | Minimum 7 Members (from diverse states for National scope) | Minimum 2 Directors/Shareholders (Private Ltd) or 7 (Public Ltd) |
| **Foundational Charter** | Trust Deed executed on stamp paper | Memorandum of Association (MoA) and Rules & Regulations | Memorandum of Association (MoA) and Articles of Association (AoA) |
| **Registering Authority** | Sub-Registrar / Charity Commissioner | Registrar of Societies (State Government) | Registrar of Companies (ROC), Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) |
| **Operating Jurisdiction** | Typically State-specific (unless registered in multi-state jurisdictions) | State-level or Multi-State / National | Nationwide and International |
| **Internal Governance** | Board of Trustees; decisions governed by Trust Deed | Governing Body / Managing Committee elected by general body | Board of Directors governed by Companies Act provisions |
| **Transparency & Scrutiny** | Low to Moderate annual reporting | Moderate annual reporting (list of managing committee, annual balance sheet) | Highest legal scrutiny, mandatory statutory audit, ROC filings |

## 2. Detailed Registration Procedures

### A. Registering as a Public Charitable Trust
1. **Drafting the Trust Deed**: State the clear charitable objectives (e.g. relief of poverty, education, medical relief, preservation of environment). Detail the powers of the Managing Trustee and rules for succession.
2. **Execution & Stamp Duty**: Execute the deed on non-judicial stamp paper corresponding to the percentage of trust property value as prescribed by the respective State Stamp Act.
3. **Sub-Registrar Submission**: The Settlor and two witnesses must physically appear before the local Sub-Registrar with verified identity and address proofs (Aadhaar, Passport, Voter ID).
4. **Grant of Registration**: Sub-Registrar issues the registered Trust Deed with a certified registration number.

### B. Registering as a Society
1. **Selection of Name**: Ensure the name does not violate the *Emblems and Names (Prevention of Improper Use) Act, 1950*.
2. **Drafting Memorandum of Association (MoA)**: Specify entity name, registered office address, detailed aims and objectives, and names, addresses, and occupations of the first governing body members.
3. **Drafting Rules and Regulations**: Specify membership criteria, quorum rules, notice periods for Annual General Meetings (AGM), tenure of office bearers, and dispute resolution mechanisms.
4. **Filing with Registrar**: Submit signed MoA, Rules & Regulations, consent letters of members, declaration by President/Secretary, and fee to the State Registrar of Societies.

### C. Registering as a Section 8 Company
1. **Name Approval**: Apply for name reservation via MCA's SPICe+ (Part A) portal.
2. **Section 8 License**: Apply for license under Section 8(1) of the Companies Act, 2013 demonstrating charitable intent and prohibition of dividend payout.
3. **Incorporation**: Submit SPICe+ (Part B) with MoA, AoA, Declarations (INC-14, INC-15), and AGILE-PRO-S for PAN, TAN, and EPFO.
4. **Certificate of Incorporation (CoI)**: Issued digitally by the Central Registration Centre (CRC) / MCA with a Corporate Identity Number (CIN).

## 3. Post-Registration Tax Exemptions & Compliance

Registration under the statutory acts creates the legal entity, but does not grant automatic income tax exemption. An NGO must separately secure:

1. **Section 12AB Registration (formerly Section 12A)**:
   - Grants exemption on charitable income, donations, and voluntary contributions from corporate income tax under Section 11 & 12 of the Income Tax Act, 1961.
   - Applications filed online using Form 10A / Form 10AB on the Income Tax e-filing portal.
   - Granted for 5 years (provisional registration for 3 years for newly established NGOs).
2. **Section 80G Certificate**:
   - Enables individual and corporate donors to claim a 50% tax deduction on their contributions.
   - Requires rigorous verification of charitable non-profit operations.
3. **Form CSR-1 (MCA)**:
   - Mandatory registration with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs for receiving Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) funds under Section 135.
4. **FCRA Registration / Prior Permission**:
   - Required before accepting any foreign funding or contributions from overseas donors.
