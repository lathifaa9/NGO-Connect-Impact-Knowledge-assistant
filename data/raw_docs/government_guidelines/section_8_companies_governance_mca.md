## 1. Statutory Provisions of Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013

A **Section 8 Company** is a non-profit company incorporated under Section 8 of the *Companies Act, 2013* with the primary objective of promoting:
- Commerce, art, science, sports, education, research, social welfare, religion, charity, protection of environment or any such other objective.

### Key Statutory Requirements:
1. **Intention to Apply Profits**: All profits, investment returns, or other income must be applied exclusively towards promoting the charitable objectives of the company.
2. **Prohibition of Dividends**: Payment of any dividend to its members/shareholders is strictly prohibited under Section 8(1)(c).
3. **Exemption from Suffix**: Unlike commercial companies, Section 8 companies are exempted from appending "Limited" or "Private Limited" to their name.
4. **Alteration of Objects**: A Section 8 company cannot alter the provisions of its Memorandum or Articles of Association respecting its charitable objects without prior approval of the Central Government (delegated to Regional Director, MCA).

---

## 2. Governance Structure and Board Responsibilities

- **Board of Directors**: Must have a minimum of 2 directors (for private limited structure) or 3 directors (for public limited structure). The requirement of independent directors under Section 149(4) does not apply to Section 8 companies.
- **Board Meetings**: Section 8 companies are required to hold at least one meeting of the Board of Directors within every six calendar months, with at least 2 meetings held annually.
- **Statutory Audit**: Financial accounts must be audited annually by an independent practicing Chartered Accountant. Audited Financial Statements (Form AOC-4) and Annual Return (Form MGT-7) must be filed with the Registrar of Companies (ROC) within 30 and 60 days of the AGM respectively.

---

## 3. Revocation of License and Penalty Provisions

Under Section 8(6) of the Act, the Central Government may revoke the license granted to the company if:
1. The company contravenes any of the requirements or conditions subject to which a license was issued.
2. The affairs of the company are conducted fraudulently or in a manner prejudicial to the public interest.

### Consequences of Revocation:
- The company will be directed to convert into a regular private or public commercial company, or
- Wound up, or
- Amalgamated with another Section 8 company having similar objects.
- Penalties: The company shall be punishable with a fine not less than ₹10 lakh, extending up to ₹1 crore, and directors in default are punishable under Section 447 (fraud).
